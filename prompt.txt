Build a single-file link-in-bio page (index.html, no build step, ready for GitHub Pages).

Content
- A round profile photo (avatar.jpg next to the file). If it is missing, show a circle with the first letter of the name.
- The name as the title and one short line under it (optional).
- Pill-shaped link buttons in this order: Instagram, Facebook, Telegram, WhatsApp, Gmail.
  Then a small divider heading "PROJECTS" and: GitHub, LinkedIn, Portfolio.
- All names, URLs and the profile text live in one clearly marked block at the top of the script
  (PROFILE and a LINKS array with title, url, icon, color and optional section), so a non-programmer can edit it.

Look
- Dark blue theme (about #050a1c to #0f2a66), light text, accent #4da3ff. Font: Poppins.
- Official brand icons as inline SVG, in their brand colours (white for GitHub, the multicolour Gmail logo).
- Buttons: glass look (semi-transparent with blur), a soft blue glow, a light sweep and a small lift on hover or focus.
- The photo has no frame or ring; on hover it lifts a little and gets a blue shadow underneath.
- Staggered fade-in when the page loads.

Background
- A 3D animated network with Three.js (pin r128 from cdnjs with an integrity hash).
  Glowing dots drift through depth, lines join dots that are close (fainter when far, brighter when near the camera),
  a few larger "hub" dots, a gentle sway, and a slight tilt toward the mouse or finger.
- Use additive blending and give the scene the same dark blue gradient as its background.
- Cap pixel ratio at 2, use fewer dots on phones, and do not rebuild the dots when only the height changes (mobile address bar).
- If Three.js or WebGL is unavailable, fall back to a flat 2D canvas network.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion (draw one still frame).

Quality
- Mobile first: 390px wide must look right, with no horizontal scroll, tap targets at least 48px high, safe-area padding.
- Links open in a new tab with rel="noopener noreferrer".
- Do not put the page gradient on <body>, because it would paint over the canvas.
